# AI adoption & operations at a CMMC compliance firm

**Role:** Contractor, moving to a full-time role: project tooling, process improvement, and AI adoption
**Organization:** A consulting firm that runs CMMC (Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification) assessments for defense contractors

> Details are kept high-level on purpose. This is a compliance-sensitive environment, and no client or internal data is shared here.

---

## The situation

Assessment work involves many hand-offs: scheduling, lead and second-chair assessors, QA review, certificates, and government reporting. These were tracked in a project tool, but roles, dates, and automations depended on people remembering the right steps. Leadership also wanted to use AI to work faster, but with the strict data rules that come with a compliance business.

## What I did

**Project tooling and process**
- Administered the firm's Wrike workspace: Gantt charts, role assignments, date-change workflows, and project blueprints.
- Wrote formal SOPs for assigning assessor roles and for changing dates on reporting and certificate tasks. I wrote them for someone who had never opened Wrike.
- Diagnosed why a set of automations stopped firing (the account had run out of automation credits). Then laid out the upgrade options and pricing, and the backfill needed for the records the automations missed.

**CRM and data**
- Backfilled HubSpot data and pulled targeted contact segments for marketing campaigns.
- Built reporting dashboards, including an AI-built SEO tracker for the firm's website ([see case study](seo-keyphrase-tracker.md)).

**AI adoption**
- Researched AI platform pricing, capabilities, and governance to support leadership's tool decision. The team standardized on one platform.
- Building reusable Claude skills and automations so the team can work more efficiently.

**Coordinating an internal AI build**
- Project-coordinating an internal self-assessment app built by the AI team.
- Acting as the go-between for the lead assessors (the subject-matter experts), the AI team, the Chief of Staff, and leadership. That means keeping requirements, decisions, and timelines moving while the experts stay focused on assessments.

## Results

- Repeatable processes are now written down, so new people can follow them without hand-holding.
- Leadership had the information it needed to choose an AI platform and set a tier for the team.
- Started as a contractor; offered a full-time role with expanded AI scope.

## What this shows

Coordinating technical and non-technical people, writing documentation people actually follow, and bringing AI into an organization where security and compliance come first.
